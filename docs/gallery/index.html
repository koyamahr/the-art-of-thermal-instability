What these recordings can tell us.

They are effective visual demonstrations at compact gallery sizes. Their native resolutions are 500–800 pixels wide and their cadences are 3–10 fps. Fine textures can look grainy, and low-frame-rate motion is visibly stepped. They are suitable as historical exhibits; they are not high-resolution, full-screen source material.

The 3D recordings carry the embedded credit “Koyama & Inutsuka 2002”. This identifies the caption on the recording; it does not establish a match to a specific paper figure, simulation dimensionality in that paper, or verified numerical parameters. The 2D titles here describe the visible panels and evolution. A number in a filename is not treated as a confirmed computational mesh size.

The gallery was curated from 87 video files, representing 55 byte-distinct recordings after SHA-256 deduplication. Astronomy candidates were visually inspected at several times. The eight selected originals and their MP4 derivatives were decoded completely; dimensions and frame counts were checked. These are technical media checks, not numerical or physical validation of the simulations.

Materials molecular-dynamics movies and related materials CG are excluded, along with unrelated footage and journal-supplement movies from other collections. Source originals remain untouched. Full-frame versions use H.264/yuv420p, retain the original dimensions and cadence, and add no spatial or temporal interpolation. The showreel has its own explicit crop and excerpt log.
