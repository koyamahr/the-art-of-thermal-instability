When a moving
biological front stops.

Bistability, diffusion and an integral selection condition also organize biological patterns. These primary-source comparisons distinguish shared mathematics from different physical mechanisms.

These are companion references, separate from the thermal-instability paper atlas. No biological simulation or quantitative cross-model reproduction is claimed. Our mathematical comparison uses the KI/ZP saturation benchmark and Elphick, Regev & Shaviv (1992).

Closest match: wave-pinning in cell polarity.

Mori, Jilkine & Edelstein-Keshet (2008), Wave-Pinning and Cell Polarity from a Bistable Reaction-Diffusion System, Biophysical Journal 94, 3684–3697, models slowly diffusing active membrane protein exchanging with a rapidly diffusing inactive pool. Total protein is conserved. An activation front consumes the pool and stops as that pool reaches a critical value.

Equations (10)–(11) and Figure 3 explicitly give a zero-reaction-integral condition, called a Maxwell condition. The near-uniform inactive concentration plays the role of a front-selection parameter; total mass also fixes the eventual front position. In ZP saturation, the selector is pressure and the integral carries the conductivity weight. This is a comparison of selection conditions, not an identification of protein diffusion with thermal conduction.

Mori, Jilkine & Edelstein-Keshet (2011), Asymptotic and Bifurcation Analysis of Wave-Pinning in a Reaction-Diffusion Model for Cell Polarization, SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 71, 1401–1427, analyzes the stationary front, its asymptotic regime and loss through bifurcations. It is the follow-up for evaluating diffusion ratios, total mass and persistence. Author-hosted published paper.

Possible small benchmark: compute the reaction integral and stationary front first, then let the conserved pool evolve. This would separate a static selector from the mechanism that drives the system toward it. No benchmark has yet been implemented.

Conserved redistribution and protein-pattern geometry.

Brauns, Halatek & Frey (2020), Phase-Space Geometry of Mass-Conserving Reaction-Diffusion Dynamics, Physical Review X 10, 041036, develops local reactive equilibria, diffusion-driven mass redistribution and stationary flux balance for two-component protein-pattern models.

Section III E, equations (16)–(20), selects stationary profiles by zero total reactive turnover. Equation (17) is a reaction-integral balance. Its interpretation as equal geometric areas between a nullcline and a flux-balance line requires additional approximations, stated before equation (19). This is especially relevant to comparing saturation conditions: identify the variable, weight and conserved quantity before drawing equal areas.

The correspondence with thermal fronts is our comparison. These multicomponent dynamics should not automatically be assigned a scalar Ginzburg–Landau free energy. Open-access original paper.

A related, distinct branch: neural excitability.

FitzHugh (1961), Impulses and Physiological States in Theoretical Models of Nerve Membrane, Biophysical Journal 1, 445–466, introduces a two-variable model of excitation and refractoriness, a foundation of the FitzHugh–Nagumo family. Its nullcline geometry and threshold dynamics provide a connection to reduced nonlinear models.

Excitable pulses involve activation followed by recovery. A cubic fast subsystem can resemble a bistable front problem when the slow variable is frozen; a full pulse is not simply a static wall between two thermally stable phases. This is a weaker analogy than wave-pinning for the present saturation-pressure question. The reference was checked at the bibliography and abstract level; its detailed equations and travelling-wave reductions have not been audited here.

What should be compared?

Retain the number of fields, the conserved quantity, the transport law, the control parameter and the reason a front stops. Protein total-mass conservation and gas mass conservation do not make temperature a conserved order parameter. A geometric resemblance between curves alone does not establish the same instability or the same stationary-front criterion.

The Elphick/Ginzburg–Landau connection supplies the reduced thermal-front functional; Maxwell’s historical construction supplies the thermodynamic comparison. These companion references extend the discussion without treating biological pattern formation as a reproduction of an interstellar calculation.
