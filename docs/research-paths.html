Research paths / Reading list

From two phases
to chemistry and cloud formation.

FGH established a two-phase picture. Later work changed the heating and chemical inputs, then followed fronts, turbulence, and magnetized clouds. This reading list connects those questions to the calculations we can document and explore.

Listed references are not completed reproductions. Bibliography and abstracts have been checked; equations, target figures, and data availability need individual review. Publication years are used where confirmed, with preprint dates noted separately.

Scope for modern simulations

Choose the scientific question before the computing budget.

Reproducing a large multidimensional simulation can require substantial computing time, storage, and access to original inputs. Our modern-paper exhibits can instead provide a source guide, documented setup, analysis of available outputs, or a small benchmark that isolates a physical mechanism.

  • Source and method: link the paper and any public code, inputs, and datasets; record what is actually available.
  • Reuse and analysis: regenerate a diagnostic from released outputs where possible, preserving units, provenance, and processing steps.
  • Small calculations: evaluate an analytic relation, a one-zone chemical model, a front, or a convergence test within an explicit reduced scope.
  • Optional full runs: record the resolution, solver, hardware, wall time, and storage needed. A full 2D/3D rerun is not a condition for completing a source or analysis exhibit.

Digitized published curves will be identified as digitizations. Reduced models will state their omissions; their results will not be labeled as full-simulation reproductions.

Hattori, Nakano and Hayashi’s reference list includes both papers. These links identify a route for source tracing; they do not establish that later models use identical coefficients.

For each future rate record, we will distinguish cross sections, rate coefficients, energy-loss coefficients, volumetric rates, and yields. Species, colliders, units, normalization, validity range, population assumptions, source equation/table, and correction/code version will accompany the numerical formula.

GOW / Code and rate provenance

Follow the corrected rates to their implementation.

The GOW paper has a 2018 erratum, and Gong’s code page lists further corrections and updates. The original paper, its erratum, a revised preprint, and an evolving code repository are separate references. Before a benchmark, we will pin the actual implementation and audit its rate definitions and units.

We recorded a public PDR-code commit for reference, but have not compiled it or validated its rates. The linked code and documentation remain maintained by their authors.

  • gow17-paper ↗ — First posted in 2016; v4 dated 2019. Published article and later corrections must be tracked separately.
  • gow17-erratum ↗ — Reaction 1 temperature exponent in the printed table is corrected. The authors state their original calculations used the correct formula.
  • gow-author-corrections ↗ — Additional rate updates and a clarification that the CR heating normalization uses the primary rate per H nucleus. This is a live page, not a frozen rate version.
  • gow-pdr-code ↗ — Commit recorded for provenance only. Not built or benchmarked here. Recorded snapshot: 2d903af68145 ↗
  • athena-chemistry ↗ — Documents GOW17 and a general KIDA interface. No Athena++ code version or network has yet been benchmarked in this archive.
