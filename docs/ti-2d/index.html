Watch an unstable medium
form cold clouds.

A real compressible-flow calculation: small density perturbations grow, pressure gradients drive motion, and cold regions condense.

Illustrative model. The KI-type heating/cooling law is coupled to deliberately enhanced, temperature-dependent conduction. This is neither a paper reproduction nor a demonstration of sustained turbulence. Cloud scales and times belong to this chosen model.

Physical time increases from 0 to 12 Myr; 241 actual snapshots, played at 15 frames/s. Bright regions have higher density. No reversed frames or artificial clouds.

Fixed logarithmic density scale: 0.15 → 40 cm⁻³ throughout. Display interpolation enlarges the 256 × 160 grid. Faint lines mark individual cells; slightly stronger guides mark every eight cells.

Equations and deliberate choices.

Periodic 4.8 × 3.0 pc box, 256 × 160 cells (Δx = Δy = 0.01875 pc). The compressible Euler equations include momentum, advection, compressional work, thermal conduction and net radiative cooling. There is no forcing, magnetic field, gravity or evolving chemistry.

γ = 5/3; m = 1.37 mH; n = ρ/m; P = nkBT. Mean density is 2.8 cm⁻³, initially at unstable thermal equilibrium. A seeded low-wave-number density perturbation has 10% RMS; temperature adjusts to keep initial pressure uniform and velocity starts at zero.

Γ = 2 × 10⁻²⁶ erg s⁻¹ per particle; Q = n²Λ(T) − nΓ. We use the corrected KI-type analytic cooling fit, evaluating its density argument as the explicitly defined n above. This is a phenomenological model with no separate ionization solution.

κ(T) = 3 × 10⁶ + 3 × 10⁸(100 K / T)² erg cm⁻¹ s⁻¹ K⁻¹. This enlarged, cold-enhanced coefficient broadens thermal interfaces for a modest grid. It differs from the Parker law and the conductivity in the KI equilibrium benchmark. Its own ZP saturation pressure and equilibrium intersections are recorded in diagnostics.json; those values must not replace the existing KI/Parker result.

The finite-volume solver uses MC-limited reconstruction, HLLC fluxes, SSPRK2, harmonic-face conductive fluxes, and explicit radiative sources. Time steps respect hyperbolic, diffusion and cooling constraints. Invalid stages cause a smaller step; there are no density or temperature floors, clipping, mass sinks or imposed condensations. The source lookup covers 2–30000 K without extrapolation.

Check the flow before trusting the picture.

The numerical report tests an analytically advected contact, continuum heat-equation decay, isochoric cooling against independent adaptive integration, and a compressible linear TI eigenmode derived from a separate Fourier-space matrix. Density, velocity and pressure are checked together. The movie run also has mass and radiative-energy accounting checks.

At 12 Myr, the cold mass fraction (T < 300 K) is 80.77%, compared with 80.93% on 128 × 80 cells. Mean P/kB is 1732.99 versus 1732.09 K cm⁻³. Maximum sampled Mach number is 0.380; the movie shows subsonic condensation.

Relative mass drift is 1.55e-14; relative radiative-energy ledger residual is 1.36e-13. No positivity retries or HLLE fallbacks occurred. Minimum sampled Field proxy is 3.98 cells on the main grid: approximately four, with a small interval below four, so a strict four-cell target is not met everywhere. The movie is an illustrative result, not a resolved-interface convergence benchmark.

The Field proxy √[κT/(n²Λ)] is an energy-balance estimate, not the exact marginal wavelength or measured interface width. Values above are sampled at history output times, not at every RK stage. A few cells in this proxy and agreement of global quantities do not establish continuum convergence of cloud morphology. The coarse grid is a sensitivity comparison, not a second physical model.

Full-grid history and coarse-grid history retain pressure, temperatures, density extrema, cold mass fraction, Mach number, resolution proxy and conservation accounting. Their global integrals use solver units over pc², per unspecified out-of-plane depth; the “mass” column integrates n and is not in grams.
